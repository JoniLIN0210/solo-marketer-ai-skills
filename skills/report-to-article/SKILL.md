# Report to article

Goal: a public article that is accurate to the source, useful on its own, and leaves enough out that the full report is still worth requesting.

## Order of authority

1. The user's request in this task.
2. The source report, the project's templates and confirmed facts.
3. The rules in this skill and [`references/configuration.md`](references/configuration.md).
4. Past articles. Reuse their structure, not their facts.

## Hard rules

- Every factual statement in the article appears in the verification table with a page or slide reference. If a claim cannot be traced, remove it, soften it, or mark it "to confirm".
- Never cite block-listed sources, including third-party reports where a block-listed organisation was a data or research partner.
- Stay within the public disclosure tier. Judge the article as a whole: several harmless numbers together can reveal what the paid report sells.
- Keep the source's scope. A regional finding stays regional; a category finding stays in its category.
- Do not add words the source does not support, such as "first", "fastest" or "leading".
- The call to action promises only what actually exists (a real form, a real report, a real contact route).

## Workflow

1. **Read everything.** Read the full report, including charts, footnotes and images. For slides and PDFs, look at each page as an image as well as the extracted text; extraction often drops chart labels and footnotes.
2. **Build a fact ledger.** For each usable fact record: page, original wording, scope (time, market, sample, unit), what it supports, and its disclosure tier. Use [`references/verification-table.md`](references/verification-table.md).
3. **Select before writing.** Choose a small number of public facts that tell one story: what is changing, the evidence, and what it means for the reader. Leave detailed breakdowns, full rankings and complete lists for the report.
4. **Write.** Lead with the change or problem the reader cares about. Then say what the report covers and move into the excerpt. Keep a neutral, informed tone. Let the evidence carry the argument instead of telling readers what to do.
5. **Verify.** Fill the "used in article" column of the ledger. Every sentence with a fact must map to a row.
6. **Package.** Deliver three files:
   - `01_<topic>_article.md`: the article.
   - `02_<topic>_verification.md`: the completed verification table.
   - `03_<topic>_titles-images-notes.md`: up to three distinct title options, image positions with page references, disclosure notes and open questions.

## Common mistakes

- Turning a regional or category result into a global claim.
- Treating a product's marketing message as a proven consumer effect.
- Mixing up growth rate, share, percentage points and index values.
- Publishing a full chart or several consecutive pages, which together replace the report.
- Writing a "hook" headline the evidence cannot support.

## Definition of done

- Every fact maps to a ledger row with a page reference.
- No block-listed source appears anywhere, including image captions.
- The article stays within the public tier when read as a whole.
- Headline, summary, body, captions and call to action use the same numbers and wording.
